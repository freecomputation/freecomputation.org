01

Our mission

The Free Computation Foundation promotes and defends the freedom to compute: to run any computation you choose, on a machine that answers to you, with results you can verify — without rent, permission, or an audience.

Computing has quietly moved house. The questions are yours; the machines increasingly are not. Work that once ran on the desk now runs in distant data centers, behind subscriptions, on devices sealed against their owners — metered, revocable, and gone the moment a vendor changes its mind. Our work is to keep computing itself free: local where it stands, exact where it counts, and owned by the person who asked the question — and by the machines that now produce and consume so much of the world's computation on their behalf.

And we are not a political movement. The Foundation takes no side in the culture wars, starts no feuds, and issues no provocations for an audience. Politics is a waste of the hours that building takes. Our whole argument is the work itself — software that exists, results that check out, knowledge anyone can use. If it cannot be run, reproduced, or read, it is not our statement.

Test 1 — The cable

"Pull the network cable."

If the tool stops working, it was never a tool. It was a service wearing a tool's clothes, and you were renting your own work back.

Test 2 — The account

"Delete the account."

If your computation dies with an account on someone else's server, the computation was permissioned — and permission can be withdrawn.

Test 3 — The proof

"Show your work."

If a result cannot be reproduced and checked by the person it is handed to, it is not a result. It is a claim, and claims are cheap.

02

The four freedoms of computation

Computation is free when every person — and every machine working for a person — holds all four of these. If any one is missing, the computing is rented, whatever anyone says about it.

0
Freedom 0

Compute without permission

Run any computation, for any purpose. No account, no activation, no authority deciding which questions your machine may answer.

1
Freedom 1

Compute on your own machine

The computation happens where you stand — locally, offline, on hardware that answers to you from the firmware up. A network is a tool, never a landlord.

2
Freedom 2

Verify what was computed

Know how a result was produced, and reproduce it exactly. Exact results, approximations, and conjectures are labelled as what they are — certainty is never manufactured.

3
Freedom 3

Share the computation

Publish programs, data, and results so anyone can run them, check them, and build on them — and keep what your tools produce. The output of a computation belongs to the one who computed it.

03

Our programs

Standing work, one per threat to free computing. These are not campaigns — each program is a thing being built, maintained, or published, and it is judged by what exists because of it.

⌂

Own Your Machine

Free computing starts below the operating system. We champion libre boot firmware and hardware that accepts its owner's instructions — machines that answer to the person at the keyboard, all the way down.

✂

Offline Is a Right

Software that dies without a network was a lease, not a tool. We build and promote offline-first computing: full capability with the cable pulled, the account deleted, the vendor gone.

=

Exact or Labelled

Results carry their epistemic rank: exact, approximate, or conjectured — never dressed as one another. We develop exact arithmetic, reproducible builds, and differential proof as everyday practice, not ceremony.

⎙

Output Is Yours

What a tool produces belongs to its user. We defend encodings, formats, and terms that attach no obligations to a computation's output.

✓

Open Proof

Research conducted in public, with artifacts anyone can check — solved, unresolved, and refuted alike, because a census is not a verification and a claim is not a certificate.

∀

Compute for Everyone

Good maths should be free. We publish tools and teaching that a student can pick up without money, permission, or a connection — free for science, in every sense of free.

04

Projects under our roof

A foundation's roof is earned by work, not filled by announcement.

None yet. On purpose.

The Foundation's first projects are being chosen deliberately, and they will appear here when they are real — working, documented, and worth your time. A listing is a claim, and we do not publish claims. When the first project takes its place here, it will be because it exists.

05

Sustained by work, not schemes

There is no membership tier, no tip jar, no sponsor button. A foundation for free computation is sustained the way free computation itself works: by people computing. Run the tools offline. Check the proofs and publish where they fail. Contribute computation, code, and counterexamples. That is the whole membership roll — everyone who computes freely is already on it. We will be judged by the work, and we intend the work to be enough.
