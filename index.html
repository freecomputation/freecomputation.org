Our mission

The Free Computation Foundation promotes and defends the freedom to compute: to run any computation you choose, on a machine that answers to you, with results you can verify — without rent, permission, or an audience.

Free software asked: may you use, study, share, and change this program? That question won, and the world runs on its answer. Free computation asks the next one: the program is free — but is the computing? When computation moves to rented clouds, sealed devices, and metered APIs, freedom can be lost without a single license changing. Software can be free while computing is not. Our work is to keep computing itself free — for people, and for the machines that now produce and consume so much of it.

We stand in the free software tradition and honor it — this foundation exists because that movement proved a freedom can be named, defended, and built. We name the next one.

And we are not a political movement. The Foundation takes no side in the culture wars, starts no feuds, and issues no provocations for an audience. Politics is a waste of the hours that building takes. Our whole argument is the work itself — software that exists, results that check out, knowledge anyone can use. If it cannot be run, reproduced, or read, it is not our statement.

The four freedoms of computation

Computation is free when every person — and every machine working for a person — holds all four of these. If any one is missing, the computing is rented, whatever the software's license says.

FREEDOM 0

Compute without permission

Run any computation, for any purpose. No account, no activation, no authority deciding which questions your machine may answer.

FREEDOM 1

Compute on your own machine

The computation happens where you stand — locally, offline, on hardware that answers to you from the firmware up. A network is a tool, never a landlord.

FREEDOM 2

Verify what was computed

Know how a result was produced, and reproduce it exactly. Exact results, approximations, and conjectures are labelled as what they are — certainty is never manufactured.

FREEDOM 3

Share the computation

Publish programs, data, and results so anyone can run them, check them, and build on them — and keep what your tools produce. The output of a computation belongs to the one who computed it.

A sibling movement, a different fight

The Free Software Foundation defends users' freedom to run, copy, distribute, study, change, and improve software. We defend a different layer of the same stack — and the difference decides what we build.

Our programs

Standing work, one per threat to free computing. These are not protest campaigns — each program is a thing being built, maintained, or published, and it is judged by what exists because of it.

Program

Own Your Machine

Free computing starts below the operating system. We champion libre boot firmware and hardware that accepts its owner's instructions — machines that answer to the person at the keyboard, all the way down.

Program

Offline Is a Right

Software that dies without a network was a lease, not a tool. We build and promote offline-first computing: full capability with the cable pulled, the account deleted, the vendor gone.

Program

Exact or Labelled

Results carry their epistemic rank: exact, approximate, or conjectured — never dressed as one another. We develop exact arithmetic, reproducible builds, and differential proof as everyday practice, not ceremony.

Program

Output Is Yours

What a tool produces belongs to its user. We defend encodings, formats, and licenses that attach no obligations to a computation's output — the principle our toolchain licenses are written around.

Program

Open Proof

Research conducted in public, with artifacts anyone can check. Our observatories publish the record — solved, unresolved, and refuted alike — because a census is not a verification and a claim is not a certificate.

Program

Compute for Everyone

Good maths should be free. We publish tools and teaching that a student can pick up without money, permission, or a connection — free for science, in every sense of free.

Projects under our roof

The foundation's own work — each project is one freedom, made concrete. All of it is built in the open at github.com/freecomputation.

Flagship

CENTL / CentL26

An exact-first, offline scientific workbench for mathematics, physics, and chemistry: exact rational arithmetic, symbolic algebra and calculus, notebooks, and a plain-English problem solver that runs entirely on your machine. Freedom 1 and 2, in daily use. Begun under NIGHTWARE LABZ; its next chapter is written here.

Language

Krell

A machine-facing portable assembly: computation designed for machines to produce and consume — typed, total, hash-consed, verifiable, and faster than C on its certified record. Its encoding exception makes Freedom 3 law: Krell-encoded programs carry no license obligations.

Research

The Erdős–Straus Observatory

An open assault on the 4/p = 1/x + 1/y + 1/z problem, classifying every artifact honestly — solved letters, incomplete residuals, and the one certified counterexample ticket that would end it. Game status: OPEN.

Sustained by work, not schemes

There is no membership tier, no tip jar, no sponsor button. Those schemes never fed the work that came before us, and we will not pretend otherwise. A foundation for free computation is sustained the way free computation itself works: by people computing. Run our tools offline. Check our proofs and publish where they fail. Contribute computation, code, and counterexamples. When the observatory calls for letters, hunt one. That is the whole membership roll — everyone who computes freely is already on it. We will be judged by the work, and we intend the work to be enough.
